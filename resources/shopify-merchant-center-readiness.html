Store launch field guide

Prepare the Shopify store before you submit it.

A careful pre-submission review reduces avoidable inconsistencies. It cannot guarantee Google approval, but it helps ensure that the website, merchant account and actual business operations tell the same story.

Discuss your store

Settle the business identity first

Confirm the store name, domain, customer-service email, business address and merchant account details. Decide which entity operates the store and ensure that public-facing disclosures accurately identify it.

Make the website complete and usable

Test navigation, product pages, cart and checkout. Ensure that contact information is easy to find and that customers can understand who they are buying from and how to get help.

Write policies from actual operations

Set shipping regions, handling times, delivery estimates, shipping charges, return windows, return costs and refund procedures before drafting policy pages. The wording must reflect the settings and practices the merchant can actually honor.

Check the catalog

Use genuine product information, accurate prices, clear availability, appropriate images and landing pages that match the feed. Remove placeholder items and test a sample of products from feed through checkout.

Compare the website and Merchant Center

Review business details, shipping settings, return information and product data side by side. A value entered in one system should not contradict the customer's experience elsewhere.

Test mobile and desktop

Check page loading, menus, policy links, product options and checkout on a phone as well as a desktop. Make sure important information is not hidden or broken at smaller screen sizes.

Preserve the submitted version

Keep a dated record of policies, business details, product feed settings and the domain configuration at the time of submission. This makes future troubleshooting more evidence-based.

Understand the limits

Google independently evaluates each merchant and may request additional information or decline approval. A clean pre-submission review is good operational practice, not a guarantee of verification.
